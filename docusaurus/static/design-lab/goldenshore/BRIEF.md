# THE GOLDEN SHORE

*Eighth commission of the Strapi Docs Design Lab. Register: photoreal presence.*

**Logline.** A golden-hour headland on the edge of a warm sea, where all 290 real pages of the Strapi documentation stand as lantern-lit waystations along a keeper's coast; walking is reading, the citation graph is the network of worn footpaths under your boots, and the light has been kept, commit by commit, since the first of March 2023.

---

## WHAT THIS PLACE IS

Every documentation corpus is secretly a settlement. People arrive not knowing where anything is, someone has to keep the roads passable, the oldest buildings hold the town's memory, and there is always one house every newcomer must be walked to first. The Golden Shore takes that truth literally. It is a terraced coastal headland at golden hour, the kind of place the best Assassin's Creed vista artists build when they want you to stop climbing and just stand there: a small harbor of old stone at the waterline, a town of plaster and terracotta rising through olive terraces, a switchback cliff road of sixty-four stations, and at the summit a lighthouse whose lamp has been relit one hundred twenty-six times.

Nothing in it is invented. The harbor is the Cloud documentation, and its five cornerstones carry the same founding date because five real pages, Cloud deployment, Cloud intro, Usage and billing, Projects overview and Project settings, all received their first commit on 2023-03-01, the oldest stones in the corpus. The cliff road is the Upgrades section, sixty-four real pages, the largest section in the taxonomy, and the marked stretch signed "v4 → v5" is exactly the four pages the taxonomy puts there. The great stone crossing where more roads converge than anywhere else on the coast is Breaking changes, because fifty-seven real pages cite it and it cites fifty-two back; no other page comes close. The lighthouse at the top is the Release notes, the most-tended page in the entire corpus at 126 commits, last relit on 2026-09-02, days ago. Walking this coast is reading the documentation. There is no other content.

## ART DIRECTION

**The honesty of the register.** True UE5 photorealism in a browser means server-side pixel streaming, which a static lab cannot host and will not fake. The honest local path is WebGL2 through Three.js r170 pushed as hard as light will carry it, and the wager of this commission is that the photograph feeling comes from light, not from asset count. Golden hour is the cheapest photorealism there is. A sun eight to eleven degrees above a real Sky.js atmosphere, turbidity tuned until the disc goes honey and the zenith stays a deep, slightly violet blue. ACESFilmic tone mapping with exposure around 0.9 so highlights roll off like film instead of clipping. Warm fog with height falloff pooling in the terraces, so every ridge line reads as a separate plane the way a telephoto AC vista does. Long shadows from a soft cascaded shadow map, tight cascade near the camera, forgiving far away. That is the whole trick, and it is enough.

**Light plan.** One directional sun, locked low over the sea to the west so the Water.js surface carries a road of glitter straight toward the harbor mouth. The Sky.js dome feeds a low-resolution environment capture once at load so PBR materials take the sky's actual color into their reflections. God rays are honest fakery: additive translucent shafts hung between the cypress rows on the cliff road, visible only when the camera looks within the sun's quadrant, faded by angle. Night never falls; the piece lives permanently in the last forty minutes of the day, because the subject of the piece is the people who keep the light on.

**Palette.** Honey limestone, warm plaster in white and ochre, terracotta roofs, dusty olive and dark cypress green, a sea of hammered gold near the sun lane and deep teal in shadow. Strapi violet #4945FF appears exactly where dye would appear in a real town: the banners of the harbor, the trim of the lanterns, the painted door of the Quick Start house. It is the accent of human care against mineral warmth, never a tint over the world.

**Materials.** PBR standard materials with procedural detail, no texture downloads: limestone and plaster get noise-derived normal maps and roughness variation generated to canvas at boot, wood gets a directional grain normal, the lighthouse lamp gets brass with high anisotropy-feeling roughness bands. Terracotta roof tiles are instanced geometry with slight per-instance hue jitter, the single cheapest way to make a roof look owned rather than tiled. Vegetation is fully instanced: olive trees, cypress, and grass cards with vertex-shader wind sway driven by a world-position phase so gusts travel across the hillside. Rocks are a small family of deformed icosahedra reused at many scales, dressed by the fog.

**Camera and finish.** First person at human eye height, gentle head bob (removed under reduced motion), 70 degree FOV. A subtle film grain and lens vignette composited in a final pass, plus depth cueing into the fog color. DPR capped at 1; the performance law is a p95 frame time at or under 33 ms, measured and reported truthfully, because a photoreal world may honestly run at 30 fps and lying about it is the one unforgivable sin in this lab.

**Type and signage.** In-world signage uses canvas-rendered textures in a humanist sans from the system stack, engraved-style for stone, painted for wood, and every sign carries only real strings: real page titles, real section names from taxonomy.json, real dates from provenance. Canvas text is scenery, never reading. Reading happens in a crisp, untinted DOM overlay, paper white in light mode, that renders the page's actual content blocks from content.json as real HTML at full text rendering quality.

## GAMEPLAY

**First paint.** You arrive standing on the pier, sun low over the water, gulls somewhere above, the sea doing its slow glitter. One quiet line rests at the bottom of the screen and then fades: "WASD to walk, mouse to look, E to read. That is all." Ahead, past the harbor gate, one house has a lit lantern and a violet door, and the pier boards, the banner line, and the light shaft through the gate all point at it. That is /cms/quick-start, the Quick Start Guide, and the level design makes it the first page every new visitor reaches, not by force but the way a good AC town leads you to the viewpoint: every other route from the pier is slightly uphill, slightly darker, slightly longer. Its door sign reads, truthfully, "19 renovations by 5 hands since 2025-02-06."

**The one verb.** Walk. Everything else is a consequence of walking. Approach any station and its sign resolves out of the haze with the real title; press E within reach and the world stops, pointer lock releases, and the reading overlay opens with the actual page: its Tldr, paragraphs, headings, code blocks, exactly the 290 pages of content.json, nothing summarized, nothing rewritten. Escape or the close button returns you to the exact spot in the exact light. Pointer lock refused or unavailable becomes click-to-walk with drag look, same coast, same verb.

**The architecture of the graph.** The 1,231 citation edges are the road network, literally. An edge between two pages is a footpath between their stations, and a page's inbound count is the wear on the ground around it: two or three links is a goat track in the grass, ten is packed dirt, twenty-five is cobbles, and the fifty-seven roads that converge on Breaking changes justify the one bridge on the coast, a stone crossing over the ravine between the town and the cliff road, the most trafficked junction in the world because it is the most trafficked junction in the corpus. Document Service API, forty-eight inbound, is the wellhouse at the center of the API terraces where nine sub-station paths radiate out. The twenty-nine edges that cross between CMS and Cloud are the harbor stairs, the only paths that touch both the waterline and the town. And the fifty real pages with zero inbound links are the unmarked crofts: standing off-trail in the olive rows, no path leads to them, and finding one is the explorer's reward the open-world genre was built on.

**Districts are taxonomy, only taxonomy.** The sixteen sections of taxonomy.json are the sixteen quarters of the coast, named on carved boundary stones with their true counts: Getting Started (19) at the harbor gate, Features (26) along the market terrace, AI (3) as three freshly plastered pavilions whose mortar still looks new because their commits are, Content APIs (43) under the long colonnade, Configurations (45) in the workshop quarter, Development (32) in the shipwright yards, TypeScript (6) a blue-shuttered row, the single CLI page a signal mast, Plugins development (28) in the artisans' lanes, Upgrades (64) as the switchback cliff road, and the six Cloud quarters down at the water. No invented groupings, no algorithmic communities, ever.

**The care act.** Every station carries a lantern, and every lantern burns with the truth of provenance.json: brightness maps to days since the page's last commit, and the engraving on the lantern's collar gives first commit, last commit, commit count, and the top keeper's name. The Media Library lantern burns bright, touched 2026-09-02; older lanterns down in the archive lanes burn low and amber. The care act is tending: stand at any lantern and hold F, and your hands cup the flame for two seconds, the glow steadies and warms, and the coast remembers you as one of its keepers. Tended lanterns are stored in localStorage behind a try/catch, glow warmer for you on every return, and appear as marks on your compass, a personal constellation of pages you have chosen to watch over. It is the same act the provenance file records 2,108 times by 77 real people, and the twelve stations whose keepers worked after midnight, the Docker house four separate nights, have visibly smoked lantern glass to prove it.

**Progression.** A leather logbook (Tab) tracks pages read per district against real totals, drawn as sixteen rows of stamps. Reading Quick Start plus at least one page in five different districts brings the keeper's hour: the sun eases two degrees lower, the light goes one stop warmer, and the lighthouse lamp becomes visible turning at the summit, an invitation to climb the sixty-four stations of the Upgrades road to the top, where the Release notes burn in the lamp room and the whole coast lays itself out below in one honest vista, every quarter, every path, every lantern.

**Reduced motion.** Honored fully: no head bob, no wind sway, no gull drift, water normals frozen to a still sheen, instant crossfades instead of moving transitions, and a district map offering direct teleport so no one is forced to traverse. The reading overlay is always static.

## STORY

They say the coast was empty until the first of March, 2023, when five stones were laid at the waterline in a single day: the harbor office, the deployment quay, the billing house, the overview terrace, and the harbormaster's own desk at Project settings. That last building has since been rebuilt seventy-eight times across 1,238 days of care, the longest tenancy on the coast, and the harbormaster's ledger does not apologize for it; a harbor that stops being repaired stops being a harbor.

The town above grew the way documentation grows, which is to say in weather. Two thousand one hundred and eight acts of care by seventy-seven keepers, most by daylight, twelve stations tended after midnight by someone who could not leave a wrong sentence standing until morning. Three hundred nineteen thousand words are carved into this hillside, and 1,343 blocks of working code are set into the walls like whetstones, put there to be used, not admired. The longest single walk is the colonnade of Understanding populate, 5,534 words end to end, and old keepers will tell you that everyone gets lost in it exactly once, which is why it was built as a colonnade: you can always see the next column.

At the ravine stands the Crossing, and the Crossing is the truest thing on the coast. Fifty-seven roads arrive at Breaking changes and fifty-two leave it, because every migration, every version, every "why did this stop working" walks through that one span of stone. The keepers re-mortared it as recently as 2026-08-31. It is not the prettiest station. It is the one the coast cannot lose.

And above it all the Golden Shore turns. One hundred twenty-six times relit, most recently four days ago, it exists for one reason, the same reason the whole coast exists: so that a stranger arriving in the dark water offshore can find the harbor mouth, tie up at the pier, and be led, gently, past the banners, to a small house with a violet door where nineteen renovations by five pairs of hands have kept one promise polished: start here, and in less time than a sunset you will have built something real. Every newcomer is walked to that door first. Every keeper remembers being walked to it. That is what the golden hour is for on this coast; it is not decoration, it is the hour when the light is low enough that you finally notice who has been keeping it.

## LAWS ACKNOWLEDGED

Every visible fact derives from the real data: 290 pages, real titles, official taxonomy names on every boundary stone, real git dates on every lantern. English only. No em dashes. No git at build time. Quick Start is the first station the level leads to. Reading is a crisp untinted DOM overlay, never canvas text. Controls are explained in-world in one quiet line. localStorage is wrapped in try/catch. Vendored Three.js r170 under the granted exception, importmap mapping "three" to ./vendor/three.module.js, no CDN, no network at runtime. Headless Chromium only for the shoot, viewport 1440x900, --use-angle=metal. p95 frame time at or under 33 ms at DPR 1, reported honestly. CHANGELOG kept in iterlog/.
