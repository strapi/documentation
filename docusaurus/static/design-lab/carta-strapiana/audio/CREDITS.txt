CARTA STRAPIANA - THE SHANTY
Credits and licences for the recorded human voices in this directory.

Every voice here is a real person singing, chanting or humming, and every
recording was released by its author under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0
Universal public domain dedication
(https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). CC0 imposes no condition
of attribution. The credit below is given anyway, because this whole piece is
about crediting the hands that made a thing.

No voice in this build is synthesised. If a synthesised voice were ever
substituted for one of these files the piece would be lying, and the docket
that commissioned it said so on the record. (Two candidate recordings were
REJECTED during this build for exactly that reason: freesound 854331, a
synthesiser wavetable wearing a yo-ho name, and freesound 385075, a music box
playing a shanty. Neither ships. Freesound 690997, a designed ship-ambience
FX bed, was rejected as not being a clean human phrase.)

WHAT WAS DONE TO THEM

Each file is ONE continuous cut of ONE recording. Nothing is pitch-shifted,
time-stretched, reversed, spliced or layered inside any file. The processing
chain, identical for every voice and reproducible by running ../make-audio.sh:

  highpass 95 Hz, lowpass 6.2 kHz   (an old deck has no top and no rumble)
  short fade in, 0.45 s fade out
  loudnorm to -22 LUFS, -3 dBTP      (so no voice shouts down another)
  22.05 kHz, mono, MP3 48 kbit/s

The first six keep 1.1 - 1.9 s of appended silence from the old loop days;
the programme no longer loops anything, so that tail is only air. Whole
bundle: seventeen files, under 1 MB, against a 3 MB ration.

THE PROGRAMME

The voices are no longer loops. They are one-shot phrases scheduled as call
and response: the lead hand calls (a call or verse phrase), the watch answers
after the call is mostly out, each answer off the beat. Voices still JOIN one
at a time at an interval computed from the real commit rhythm of the island
being crossed to, and the chorus never grows past the number of hands that
actually keep that page. Between phrases there is silence - the wind and
water carry those bars, and they are the whole piece if no voice ever loads.

Laws, every play logged in the page (window.__helmSound.trig) so they can be
proved rather than promised:
  - never the same phrase twice in a row, anywhere in the programme;
  - no phrase-to-phrase sequence (A then B) heard again within ten minutes;
  - every play gets a new small pitch (within about a third of a semitone)
    and a new small level, so no two plays are identical;
  - while the reading pane is open the whole mix eases to one quarter and
    the programme keeps running behind the duck.

THE BED

The wind and the water under the voices are NOT recordings. They are
synthesised in the page with the Web Audio API: one looping noise buffer split
into a band-passed wind and a low-passed sea, both breathing on slow
oscillators tied to the swell and to the ship's actual speed through the
water. That bed is always on, it is the whole sound if every voice fails to
load, and one switch (S, or the control on deck) silences everything at once.
The one arrival one-shot - the anchor cable running out and the splash - is
synthesised the same way; it is rigging, not a voice.

THE SUNO SLOT

The owner may commission verses of his own. Drop MP3 files into audio/suno/
next to a manifest.json shaped like this:

  {
    "verses": [
      { "file": "verse-one.mp3",
        "title": "The Ballad of the Chart Table",
        "by": "Suno, commissioned by the owner",
        "gain": 0.55 }
    ]
  }

file is the MP3 inside audio/suno/ (required); title and by feed the credit
line shown on the plate when the verse plays; gain (0..1, default 0.55) sets
its level. When the folder and manifest exist they are detected at load, and
the verses are woven into the programme as FEATURED VERSES: now and then the
crew stands silent, the verse takes the deck alone, and the plate names it -
title and hand, the same credit law as every voice above. No manifest, no
change: the bank above carries the watch.
