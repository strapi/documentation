CARTA STRAPIANA - THE SHANTY
Credits and licences for the recorded human voices in this directory.

Every voice here is a real person humming, and every recording was released by
its author under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal public domain
dedication (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). CC0 imposes no
condition of attribution. The credit below is given anyway, because this whole
piece is about crediting the hands that made a thing.

No voice in this build is synthesised. If a synthesised voice were ever
substituted for one of these files the piece would be lying, and the docket
that commissioned it said so on the record.

WHAT WAS DONE TO THEM

Each file is ONE continuous cut of ONE recording. Nothing is pitch-shifted,
time-stretched, reversed, spliced or layered inside the file. The processing
chain, identical for every voice and reproducible by running ../make-audio.sh:

  highpass 95 Hz, lowpass 6.2 kHz   (an old deck has no top and no rumble)
  0.22 s fade in, 0.45 s fade out
  1.1 - 1.9 s of silence appended    (so a looping voice draws breath)
  loudnorm to -22 LUFS, -3 dBTP      (so no voice shouts down another)
  22.05 kHz, mono, MP3 48 kbit/s

Total: six files, about 363 KB. MP3 was chosen over Ogg Vorbis or Opus only
because every browser's decodeAudioData reads it.

THE BED

The wind and the water under the voices are NOT recordings. They are
synthesised in the page with the Web Audio API: one looping noise buffer split
into a band-passed wind and a low-passed sea, both breathing on slow
oscillators tied to the swell and to the ship's actual speed through the water.
That bed is always on, it is the whole sound if these six files fail to load,
and one switch (S, or the control on deck) silences everything at once.

HOW THEY ARE USED

One voice starts when the ship has way on her. The others JOIN, one at a time,
at an interval computed from the real commit rhythm of the island being crossed
to: an island worked often takes up the chant quickly, a page touched twice in
four years is slow to answer. The chorus never grows past the number of hands
that actually keep that page. At anchor the voices fall away.
