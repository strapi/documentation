THE DOG, RECAST (round 7)
-------------------------
The owner rejected the dog this build used to carry ("change le son choisi
pour le chien, ca ne va pas du tout lol - cree plusieurs types de sons pour
ce chien"). Those three files - a bark and a grunt from OpenGameArt's "Dog
sounds" and "Dog grunt", and a cloth-shake from Kenney RPG Audio - are
DELETED from this build, not silenced in it.

In their place: six voices, sourced fresh, auditioned before wiring
(waveform and spectrogram inspected for every candidate) and rejected on
sight if they read as a growl, a big dog or an animal in distress. All six
are peak-normalised to the same -9 dBFS ceiling; the build then plays each
of them at half the gain the mix first reached for, and clamps every one of
them below the peak of the loudest gust. She is never louder than the wind.

ROUND 17 - THE LAW MADE TRUE AS DECODED. Low-bitrate opus had moved each
file's decoded peak down by a different amount (measured 0.311-0.352
against the declared 0.355), so the levelling above was only true before
the encode. Every dog file is now re-scaled iteratively until the peak A
DECODER MEASURES lands in 0.343-0.351 - at or under the 0.355 the mix
arithmetic assumes, the whole set inside a 0.011 spread. Pure gain, no
re-cut. dog_bark.ogg additionally pitched up a further 35 % (asetrate
64800 -> 48000): fundamental ~445 -> ~600 Hz, energy-weighted centroid
571 -> 786 Hz, duration 0.300 -> 0.222 s - a smaller dog yet, same
recording, same source and licence.

ROUND 11 - AND THE WIND ITSELF CAME DOWN. The gust was still the loudest
ambient voice in the build (0.246 file peak x 0.16 = 0.0394 of full scale),
above the crickets at 0.0230 and the rain at 0.0259 - weather shouting
over weather. It is trimmed by a further 0.48 to
0.0189, a hair under the wind bed's own 0.0202, so the two halves of the
wind are now the quietest layers on the trail. The dog follows it down,
because the law is that she is never louder than it: her ceiling is that
same 0.0189, and her six gains were halved again to sit under it (measured
peaks 0.0135 to 0.0178). The walker's snore and the dog's answer to the
same ceiling (0.0171 and 0.0111). Every one of these numbers is asserted,
not asserted-to: qa/r11-levels.mjs decodes each file, multiplies by the
gain the build actually plays it at, and orders the whole mix.

SHE IS SILENT WHILE YOU WALK, AND ROUND 12 IS THE ROUND THAT MADE THAT TRUE.
Rounds 10 and 11 wrote this paragraph and the build did not keep it: eight
ordinary walking minutes measured 65 dog sounds, 8.1 a minute, because the
snuffle was gated only per-door. The corpus holds 522 doors past sniffMin
spread over 232 of the 290 stretches, so at a walking stride she met one
every few seconds and the gesture became a tic.

The rule now lives in one function, stoppedTogether(), and EVERY voice of
hers - the pant included - reads it before it sounds: SHE HAS STOPPED AND
YOU HAVE STOPPED WITH HER (S.idleT > 1.2 s - no walking, no click-to-walk under way,
no overlay). Under it sits a small floor: no two of her sounds within 4-9 s of each
other, the gap drawn fresh each time. The floor is NOT where her rarity
lives - that is the stop rule and the five per-voice clocks below. All it
does is stop two genuine events landing on the same instant. A long floor
would have one event gagging another, which is worse; and a gesture bad
timing lands on is not spent. Round 13 made that true in BOTH directions:
whether it is the floor that says no, or the stop itself that has not
finished - she outruns you, so at a door you stop at together her nose
arrives half a second before your 1.2 s of stillness clears, which round
12 silently spent onto the door's cooldown (the verifier stood at a
well-cited door and heard nothing) - the snuffle is HELD, and given the
moment you are both properly standing there. The hold lives twelve
seconds, and only while the stop is real: you within 460 px of the door,
her still at it. Walking past a door kills the hold inside a stride and a
half, so an ordinary walking minute still measures zero - the round-13
fix was measured against the same eight-minute walk that convicted round
11 (0 sounds, 8/8 minutes silent, qa/iv2rb-dogwalk.mjs), and the held
snuffle against a real arrow-key walk-up (qa/r13-stopsniff.mjs: silent
walk, ONE snuffle, voiced at idle 1.84 s with the legs still).
Her nose still works while you
walk - she runs ahead and reads the door exactly as before - but she does it
without a word. The table below is the code, not an intention:

    dog_sniff  her nose has reached a door that M.sniffMin or more pages
               cite, AND YOU HAVE STOPPED THERE WITH HER - the word held,
               if need be, until your stillness clears 1.2 s and the floor
               lifts. That door again: 45 s.
               Any snuffle at all: 90-150 s, drawn fresh.
    dog_yip    the same event at a rarer door - one in the top fiftieth of
               cited pages (M.yipMin). She gives the yip INSTEAD of the
               snuffle, when her nose comes up: an investigation is only
               ever one sound.
    dog_pant   the rarest of them: NOT trotting - a door, a fast travel or
               the index has opened more than 900 px of daylight between you
               and she has run it down, and you are standing where the travel
               put you when she arrives. Walking beside you never opens such
               a gap. Once per ~2 minutes at the very most, and only about a
               run she had in the last 12 s. (Round 12 took away this voice's
               exemption from the stop: a probe that held an arrow key THROUGH
               a teleport heard her puff three times in eight minutes, which
               was the last noise in the build made with the legs moving.)
    dog_sigh   she has settled - sitting or asleep - within 170 px of you,
               after 25 s of your stillness (once per 75-140 s; round 12
               stretched it from 48-92 s, where it was her most frequent
               voice at a standstill)
    dog_shake  a genuine mist (an uncited stretch) or a genuine shower, once
               per wet stretch, at least 90-135 s since the last shake, and
               she plants her feet to do it - the shake, too, waits for the
               stop (round 11 held the cooldown; round 12 added the stop)
    dog_bark   THE ONE VOICE THE OWNER KEPT FOR THE WALK ITSELF - "sauf
               rarement de temps en temps un petit aboiement". Her own
               clock, 170-300 s, randomised, never twice in a row, never
               while she or the walker sleeps, never at the shore. She
               plants her feet to give it: a dog at a dead run keeping her
               lead on you has neither the breath for it nor anything to
               bark at, so the clock RIPENS while you walk and the bark
               lands at your next real pause (idle > 2.5 s).

Every voice she gives is written to AUD.dogTrace with the walker's idle
clock and speed at that instant, readable at window.__aud.state.dogTrace, so
no future round has to take this paragraph on trust: qa/r12-dogwalk.mjs and
qa/r12-dogwalk2.mjs read it back.

The weather and the sleeper answer to the same rule - a countable event each:
    rain       a shower is actually passing over the stretch you stand on
    snore      the walker has been still for two minutes and is properly
               asleep (and once in a while the dog, a fifth higher)
Pitch and timing shift a little on every play, so she never repeats herself.
At Land's End she is quiet: the shore keeps its silence.
